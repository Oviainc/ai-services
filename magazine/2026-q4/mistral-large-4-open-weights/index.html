Open Weights, Open Operations: Mistral Large 4

A trillion-parameter model became an asset a team can hold: what open weights change for sovereignty, economics and operations.

Ovia Inc · Studio and Lab · 5 min read

On Thursday, October 29, 2026, Mistral released the weights of Mistral Large 4, a natively multimodal mixture-of-experts model of one trillion parameters, with roughly 49 billion parameters active on each token. The weights are published on Hugging Face, free to download and run. A model of that scale leaving the building on purpose, handed to anyone with the infrastructure to run the model, is the story: capability that had lived behind an API became an asset a team can hold, inspect, and operate.

By Friday the release was the top AI story on Hacker News with 1,472 points, a measure of how closely the developer world watches the open-weights frontier. Attention alone proves little, but the scale of the attention tells us where practitioners believe the centre of gravity is moving. Each major open release resets expectations about what a team can run without permission, and Large 4 arrived at a size that would recently have been assumed to stay closed.

We track open models the way operators track weather: what changed, what running the model costs, and what the model unlocks for agents in production. Large 4 gives that tracking a concrete subject, because the release joins three threads at once: sovereignty, economics, and craft. The weights are free; running the weights well is not, and the difference between those two facts is where the real work now sits.

01 · The release

The release itself is simple to state and large in consequence. A natively multimodal mixture-of-experts model, one trillion parameters in total, became downloadable. Mixture-of-experts design is part of why the numbers can coexist: roughly 49 billion parameters active on each token means the full trillion does not fire for every word, so capability and cost can be balanced in a way dense models cannot match. Multimodal input means the same asset can work across text and images, which matters for agents that read documents, screens, and scenes as part of a task.

02 · Why open weights change the operating picture

A downloaded model is an asset, not a service. The model runs where the team runs it, keeps working if a vendor changes strategy, and can be inspected, fine-tuned, and frozen at a known version for regulated work. Freezing matters more than novelty in many production settings: a known version can be evaluated once, approved once, and defended later, while a hosted model can shift underneath an application without warning.

Trained in Europe and framed by its maker as sovereign infrastructure, Large 4 also lands in a policy moment: governments and enterprises increasingly ask where a model lives and who can switch the model off. Open weights answer both questions in one move: the model lives here, and nobody can switch the model off from elsewhere. For teams handling sensitive material, that answer changes procurement conversations as much as technical ones, because control over the artefact becomes part of the security case.

03 · The economics move in-house

Mistral reports that Large 4 ties a leading competitor on a coding-agent index while using 47% fewer tokens per task, with an API price of $0.70 per million input tokens and $2.10 per million output tokens for those who prefer the hosted route. Those performance figures are vendor-reported and await independent validation. The hosted price still matters as a reference point: a hosted route sets the price that self-hosting has to beat, once hardware, power, and operations time are counted honestly.

The structural point stands regardless of the headline figures: when the weights are free, the differentiator becomes operations: quantisation choices, serving stacks, batching, caching, and evaluation. Cost per correct answer becomes a craft metric, and craft can be learned, certified, and improved. That pattern is familiar from our own training ground: the Hugging Face Agents and Deep RL certifications from May 2025 teach agents as systems to build and evaluate, and the context engineering work at Ovia since December 2022 treats every model, open or hosted, as one component inside a larger operational design.

04 · The fine print

Open weights are not the same as an unrestricted licence. Mistral terms allow broad use of the weights, while commercial redistribution of the model as a hosted service requires a separate licence. Teams planning products on top of Large 4 should read the licence before designing the business model, not after. A product built on a reading of the licence that turns out to be wrong is not a minor legal detail; the licence can decide whether the product can exist in the planned form at all.

Provenance matters too: a trillion-parameter download deserves the same supply-chain discipline as any other critical dependency: verified sources, pinned versions, tested updates. Checksums, mirrors, and evaluation before promotion belong in the runbook from the first download. Openness removes the gatekeeper; openness does not remove the need for care, and in some ways openness raises the standard, because the team holding the weights also holds the responsibility.

05 · What to watch

Three signals will show whether Large 4 matters beyond a headline week: independent benchmark replication of the efficiency claims; the first wave of fine-tunes and agent frameworks adopting the model as a default open option; and enterprise deployments that choose the model for sovereignty reasons rather than price. Fine-tunes are an especially honest signal, because a fine-tune represents real investment by a team that has handled the weights and decided the model is worth building on.

At Ovia, open models are tracked the way operators track weather: what changed, what running the model costs, and what the model unlocks for agents in production. The wind is real; the craft now is navigation.

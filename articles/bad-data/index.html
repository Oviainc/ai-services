Bad Data: How Poor Quality Data Can Impact Your Bottom Line

Bad data bills everyone, twice.

The first bill arrives as a wrong number in a report — a decision made on data that was incomplete, duplicated, or already out of date. The second bill arrives later, and larger: the rework, the correction, and the trust that has to be rebuilt with everyone who acted on the first number.

That lesson sits at the centre of the business intelligence work behind Ovia: years of data and automation work teach one order of operations — clean the data first, govern the definitions second, automate only after both hold.

Agents make the order more important, not less. A dashboard with bad data misleads one reader at a time. An agent acting on bad data repeats the error at machine speed.

We clean the data before we automate anything.

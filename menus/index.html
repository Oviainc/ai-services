THE CONCEPT

Every menu can be read twice. First as dinner — twelve dishes cooked from whatever the season is offering, roots and smoke in autumn, blossom and green in spring. Then again, underneath, as an essay: each dish translates one idea from the frontier of intelligence into flavour. A world model hides in roasted squash. Quantization hides in a spiced chai. Distillation arrives as a broth so clear it looks like water and tastes like a forest. Taken in order, the courses tell one story — how anything, kitchen or machine, learns to perceive, to remember, and to reason.

The four seasons share a single skeleton — black on white, the same calm measure — and part company only in temperament. Spring is light and high-contrast, set in a Didone hand. Summer opens into a rounder Palatino. Autumn, where the series began, keeps the house serif. Winter turns spare: small caps, roman numerals, room to linger. Different hands, one family. Open any dish and the photograph takes the whole screen; zoom, and the detail holds — because the picture was never small to begin with.

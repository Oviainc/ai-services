The Manifesto

Principles behind the OVIA Manifesto. We follow these principles:

Our highest priority is to satisfy the client through the continuous running of a working operation — agents, machine learning and product, live in the business and kept live, not delivered and left.

Welcome changing requirements, even late in an engagement. An operation built on engineered context absorbs change for the client's competitive advantage; one that cannot change cannot improve.

Deliver working systems early, then keep them running in short cycles of measurement and adjustment, with a preference for the shorter timescale. Proof in production outranks promises in a presentation.

Clients and the team that serves them work together throughout the engagement. Nothing is handed over and abandoned, so there is no distance for the work to fall into.

Build operations around the people who run the business. Give them systems that carry the repetitive weight, an environment that supports those systems, and trust both to get the job done.

The most efficient and effective method of conveying information to and within a client's business is a conversation in the client's own language, in the channels the business already uses. Multilingual is the standard, never a feature.

A running operation is the primary measure of progress. Not documents, not demonstrations, not a launch date — the operation, working, today.

Our processes promote a sustainable pace. The sponsors, the team and the clients should be able to maintain the arrangement indefinitely: everything contracted in writing at a fixed price, a named person responsible, and every serious inquiry answered within one business day.

Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design keeps an operation trustworthy. Context, carefully engineered and kept current, is what every agent runs on.

Simplicity — the art of maximizing the amount of work the client no longer has to do — is essential.

The best operations emerge from one senior team that designs, builds and runs its own work: a studio that designs, a lab that proves, and no handover between them.

At regular intervals, the operation is measured against its purpose, the team reflects on what the measurements say, and then tunes and adjusts the system accordingly. What gets measured gets better, and the library of what is learned compounds.

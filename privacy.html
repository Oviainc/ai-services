Privacy & AI Transparency

This page is published in English first; the French and Spanish versions follow. The promises in it apply in every language.

The short version

Your data is used only for your business. It is never sold, never shared for anyone else's benefit, and never used to train public AI models. Modern work uses AI tools — including, at times, models from providers such as OpenAI — and this page says exactly what that means, what is sent, what is not, and what control you keep. Ask anything, at any time: a straight answer is part of the service.

1. What this page is

This page covers the OVIA Inc. website and OVIA's client work: what information is collected, why, who touches it, which third-party tools are involved, and the rights and choices you keep throughout. It is written to be read, not filed. Where a signed engagement's written terms say something more specific, those terms govern.

2. What the website collects

The contact form. Name, contact details, the service selected and the message written. The form is processed by Formspree and delivered to OVIA as email. It is used to answer the inquiry — nothing else.

Analytics. The site runs Google Tag Manager and Google Analytics to count visits and understand which pages are read. Analytics data is aggregate; it is not used to identify individual visitors, and no advertising profiles are built from it.

Nothing else. The site sets no accounts, takes no payments, and asks for no information beyond the form.

3. What clients provide during an engagement

Projects run on the client's own material: records, documents, call and message content, customer lists, system access. That material is provided for one purpose — the client's project — and it is used for that purpose only. Access is limited to the team members working on the engagement, and only to what the work requires.

4. The promises that do not bend

Client data is never sold.

Client data is never shared with anyone except the processors a project actually needs (section 5), and never for another client's benefit. One client's material is never used to help, train or inform another's.

Client data is never used to train public AI models.

Ownership is agreed in plain writing before work starts: what is the client's stays the client's, including the data, the content provided, and the deliverables paid for.

5. AI tools and third parties — the transparency section

Honest work in 2026 uses AI. Rather than hide that, here is how it works.

Providers. Depending on the project, work may use AI model providers — for example OpenAI — alongside telephony, cloud hosting, email/form processing and analytics providers. The exact set depends on what a project needs; the categories never change silently.

What is sent. Only what a task requires. Where a step can be done without personal information, it is. Where personal information is not needed in identifiable form, it is removed or masked before processing wherever practical.

How it is sent. Business and API arrangements are used — not consumer accounts — under terms where the provider processes inputs to return outputs and does not train its public models on them.

Your right to know. On request, at any time — before signing, mid-project, or after — you receive the list of tools and providers that touch your data, and what each one receives.

Your right to restrict. You may set limits in writing before work starts: for example, no external AI processing on specified data, redaction before any external step, or processing kept to specified tools or self-hosted/open models. Those limits are then part of the engagement's terms.

Substitutions. If a provider a project depends on changes its terms in a way that affects your data, you are told, and the affected step is paused until you decide how to proceed.

Issues that apply to every tool on this list — and the standing answers: (1) Terms change. Providers rewrite policies; the substitution rule above pauses affected steps until you decide. (2) Legal process. A court can compel a provider to preserve or produce data regardless of any policy; data minimization is the real defence, because what was never sent cannot be produced. (3) People and keys. Most real incidents start with a compromised account or key, not a model: scoped, revocable credentials and need-to-work access are the controls that matter. (4) Output error. A model can produce a wrong answer with total confidence; consequential outputs are reviewed by a person (section 10).

Where data is processed

Processing happens where the tools run — Canada, the United States or the European Union, depending on the tool. The written plan for a project names the set, and with it the regions involved. Cross-border transfers ride on the providers' business terms and data-processing agreements. If your data must stay in a chosen region, say so before work starts: that requirement goes into the engagement's terms, and the tool set is built to honour it — for example, European providers or local models, whose data never leaves the premises at all.

6. When the work speaks to your customers

Some projects answer a client's own customers — calls, messages, bookings. In that work OVIA acts on the client's instructions, as their processor: the client remains responsible for their own customer notices and consents, and the data flows are set out in the engagement's written plan so both sides know, before launch, exactly what a caller or writer's information does and where it goes.

7. Security practices

Encryption in transit for data moving between systems.

Access on a need-to-work basis, limited to the engagement's team.

Backups that are checked, not just scheduled, for systems OVIA manages.

Safeguards scaled to the sensitivity of the material involved.

No system is risk-free, and this page will not pretend otherwise. If an incident affects your data, you are informed promptly, told what is known and what is being done, and the response is handled with you — not around you.

8. Retention and return

Project material is kept only as long as the work and ordinary legal obligations require. At the end of an engagement, your material is returned or exported in usable form on request, and OVIA's working copies are deleted on the schedule agreed in writing.

9. Your questions and rights

Ask questions at any point — before, during or after an engagement — and expect a straight, specific answer, including “which tools touched my data” and “show me what you hold”. Subject to the law that applies to your business — including Québec's Law 25 and Canada's PIPEDA where they apply, the GDPR and the UK GDPR for clients and visitors in Europe and the United Kingdom, and the privacy law of your own jurisdiction — you may request access to, correction of, or deletion of personal information OVIA holds for you, and those requests are honoured within the timelines the law sets.

10. The honest limits of AI

AI systems can be wrong — confidently wrong. Consequential outputs in OVIA's work are reviewed by a person before they are relied on, and final decisions remain the client's. Nothing on this site or in any deliverable guarantees a business outcome.

11. Changes to this page

If this page changes in a way that affects how client data is handled, the change is posted here with its date, and clients with active engagements are told directly.

12. Ask

Every engagement starts with a conversation. Bring the data questions with the business ones — they are welcome, they are expected, and they will be answered in the same plain language as this page.
